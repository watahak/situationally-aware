Unlike equity pairs, GLD/TLT is not subject to passive index reflexivity or CTA equity trend mechanics. The extreme Z-score range (5.9 standard deviations in 6 months) reflects regime-switching behavior: rapid rotation between gold preference and bond preference based on macro rate expectations.

The Jun crash (ratio dropped to 4.33) and subsequent recovery to 4.86 suggests a temporary flight from gold into duration that quickly reversed. Positive dealer gamma on SPY (*Tactical — as of 2026-10-07*) suppresses equity volatility, which reduces demand for both safe havens — consistent with the joint sell-off visible in the heatmap (GLD -13.4%, TLT -7.9% over 6M). When equity volatility is dampened, the urgency to hold safe havens declines.

**2. CASH-FLOW ANCHOR — Real-rate repricing confirmed by TIP divergence**

GLD and TLT have no GDP anchor — these are real vs nominal safe haven assets. The cash-flow lens here is replaced by the TIP diagnostic.

TIP heatmap: 6M -2.6%, 1M -1.7%. TLT heatmap: 6M -7.9%, 1M -3.2%.

TLT is falling substantially more than TIP across all timeframes. Per the diagnostic framework: **TIP holding while TLT falls = real-rate repricing (Fed policy driven)**. The market is repricing the term premium on long-duration nominal bonds, not inflation expectations. If this were an inflation scare, TIP would fall more (rising inflation expectations reduce TIP's relative appeal). Instead, TIP's modest decline (-2.6% vs TLT's -7.9%) confirms that inflation expectations are relatively stable — the move is about the real rate path and duration risk premium.

For gold: the -13.4% decline is larger than what real-rate repricing alone would explain. Gold's sell-off likely also reflects reduced demand for inflation hedges (consistent with stable inflation expectations) and reduced safe-haven demand as equity volatility remains suppressed by positive dealer gamma.
